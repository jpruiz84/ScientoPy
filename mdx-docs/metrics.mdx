# The indicators

Every ranking ScientoPy prints carries five numbers per topic. Four of them
depend on a **trailing window** whose width you control with `--windowWidth`.
Getting these right is the difference between "this keyword is popular" and
"this keyword is taking off".

## The window

The window is always the **last `windowWidth` years of the analysis range**:

```python
endYearIndex   = len(years) - 1                    # the last year in range
startYearIndex = endYearIndex - (windowWidth - 1)  # windowWidth years back
```

So `--startYear 2010 --endYear 2025 --windowWidth 2` gives a window of
**2024–2025**. The default width is `2`. ScientoPy prints the resolved window
above every table, and you should quote it whenever you quote an AGR:

<Terminal title="output header">
{`Average Growth Rate (AGR) and Average Documents per Year (ADY) period: 2024 - 2025`}
</Terminal>

## Definitions

### Total

Documents matching the topic within the year range. A paper listing both
"BLE" and "Internet of Things" counts once for each — these columns do not sum
to the corpus size.

### AGR — Average Growth Rate <Badge>docs / year</Badge>

The mean **year-over-year change** in document count across the window.

```python
rate[i] = count[i] - count[i-1]          # rate[0] = count[0]
AGR     = mean(rate[startIdx … endIdx])
```

For *Bluetooth Low Energy* with a 2024–2025 window: 2024 had 110 papers against
109 in 2023 (`+1`), 2025 had 92 against 110 (`−18`), so
`AGR = (1 + (−18)) / 2 = −8.5`.

Positive means accelerating, negative means contracting, and the magnitude is in
documents per year. Because it is a first difference, AGR is **volume-sensitive**:
a huge topic losing 5% outranks a small topic doubling. That is the trade-off
`--trend` accepts in exchange for a growth signal that is not dominated by noise
on 3-paper topics.

### ADY — Average Documents per Year <Badge>docs / year</Badge>

```python
ADY = mean(count[startIdx … endIdx])
```

Current publication rate. Unlike AGR this is a *level*, not a *change*: it
answers "how active is this topic right now?" It is the default Y axis of the
[evolution plot](./graphs.mdx#evolution); `--agrForGraph` swaps in AGR instead.

### PDLY — Percentage of Documents in the Last Years <Badge>%</Badge>

```python
PDLY = 100 * sum(count[startIdx … endIdx]) / Total
```

What fraction of a topic's entire history happened inside the window. This is
the most intuitive of the four and the one that best survives being quoted
without its window.

A worked reading, at width 2 on the corpus above:

| Topic | Total | PDLY | Reading |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| *Bluetooth Low Energy* | 1,401 | 14.4% | Mature. Two of sixteen years hold a seventh of its output — roughly what an even spread would give. |
| *Bluetooth Low Energy (BLE)* | 454 | 33.0% | Concentrating. A third of all its papers are from the last two years. |
| *accuracy* | 48 | 81.2% | Brand new. Essentially did not exist before the window. |

PDLY is also what the [`bar_trends`](./graphs.mdx#bar_trends) plot annotates on
each bar, which makes it the natural indicator for a single summary figure.

### h-index

Standard definition, computed per topic over the papers matched **within the
year range**: the largest *h* such that *h* of them have at least *h* citations
each.

<Callout type="warning" title="Three things about the h-index here">
It is **not** windowed — the slider above leaves it alone. It uses the
**post-deduplication averaged** citation count, so a paper found in both
databases contributes the mean of its two counts. And for WoS records the count
comes from `Z9` (total times cited across all indexes), not `TC` (Core
Collection only), which makes it run slightly higher than a WoS-only reading.
</Callout>

## Percentage mode: `--pYear`

`--pYear` rescales the per-year counts into *percentage of all documents
published that year*, which removes the growth of the field itself from the
picture — useful when total output is rising so fast that every topic looks like
it is growing.

<Callout type="danger" title="--pYear rescales after the indicators are computed">
AGR, ADY, PDLY and Total are calculated from **raw counts**, and only then are
the per-year columns divided by that year's document total. So in a `--pYear`
run the table's indicator columns and its year columns are in different units.
The plot and the year columns show shares; AGR and ADY are still documents per
year. Don't mix them in one sentence.
</Callout>

## Choosing a window width

| Width | Good for | Cost |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `1` | "What happened last year." AGR becomes a single year-over-year delta. | Maximally noisy; one quiet year flips the sign. |
| `2` *(default)* | Trend detection on a normal-sized corpus. Matches the published methodology. | Still sensitive to a single incomplete year. |
| `3`–`5` | Small corpora, or fields with lumpy conference cycles. | Blurs recent inflections. |
| `> 5` | Long-run structure. | AGR stops meaning "recent growth" in any useful sense. |

Minimum is `1`; anything lower is rejected. There is no upper guard — a width
larger than your year range simply clamps to the whole range, and the reported
window in the header will tell you that happened.

<Callout type="tip" title="Always publish the window">
"IoT grew at AGR 6.5" is not a reproducible claim. "IoT: AGR 6.5 documents/year
over 2024–2025, from a 2010–2025 corpus of 5,524 deduplicated documents" is. The
window appears in the printed header for exactly this reason.
</Callout>
